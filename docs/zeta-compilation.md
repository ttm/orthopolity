# Published exponents of additive quantities across systems

Compiled 8 October 2026 to test whether $\zeta=1$ (equal resource per log-size class) clusters by
system class. Exponents are survival (Zipf/Pareto) exponents of the listed quantity; conversions
are $\zeta=\alpha-1$ from a density exponent and $\zeta=1/r$ from a rank exponent.

**Status.** Values were checked against search-engine extracts of abstracts, tables and secondary
summaries; full texts could not be opened from the compiling environment. *Verified* means confirmed
that way; *memory* means not confirmed in this session. Treat every value as provisional until
checked against the primary source. One representative value per system was chosen by the compiler,
and several rows reuse the same datasets. This is a scoping table, not a meta-analysis.

## Selection notes

How the systems were chosen. I started from the two standardized compilations to limit cherry-picking: Clauset, Shalizi & Newman (2009), with 24 datasets, and Newman (2005), with 12. I then added every system named in the task, plus systems I expected to be informative either way: European firms, natural cities, mutual funds, trees, the galaxy stellar mass function, dark-matter subhalos, tropical-cyclone PDI, rain, neuronal avalanches, bolides, rock fragmentation, superflares and volcanoes. Every contradicting system I found is kept.

Verification limits in this environment:
- WebFetch failed on DNS for every domain, and the outbound proxy allowed only GitHub and PyPI, so arXiv, publisher and PMC pages could not be opened directly.
- All verification therefore came from web-search result extracts. These often quote primary text (abstracts, figure captions, table fragments) and sometimes reliable secondary summaries.
- Clauset's Table 6.1 could not be retrieved whole. These rows were verified:
  - terrorism, directly;
  - citations 3.16 +/- 0.06 and protein interaction 3.1 +/- 0.3, directly;
  - wars, blackouts, cities, fires, flares, earthquakes and metabolic degree, through the comparison table in Aschwanden & Scholkmann (2025);
  - Moby Dick, through the poweRlaw documentation.
- These Clauset rows are from memory and marked verified=false: web links (tentative), Internet AS degree, email, species per genus, bird sightings, book sales, religions, surnames 2.5, net worth 2.3, papers authored, web hits 1.81, HTTP.
- Some rows mix verified and unverified numbers. In those, the unverified sub-values are labeled in the text, and the flag reflects the headline value.

Conversions:
- A density exponent alpha gives zeta = alpha - 1. A rank exponent r gives zeta = 1/r.
- For mass from linear size, zeta_m = D/3 (fragments) or 11/8 - 1 (trees, via M proportional to D^(8/3)).
- For rupture area, zeta_A = b, assuming M0 proportional to A^(3/2). For seismic moment, zeta_M0 = beta.
- For Schechter functions, zeta = -(alpha_Schechter + 1) below M*.
- For NBSS slope s, zeta = -s. For abundance per log bin slope s_N, zeta = -s_N.
- Ambiguous conventions are flagged: oil fields (D), islands, river basins, landslide subsets.

Known selection biases:
1. Selection on fame. Firms, cities, words and the Sheldon spectrum are studied because they are famous for Zipf or a slope of -1. Systems with exponential or lognormal tails are rarely written up as power laws, so publication favors heavy tails.
2. Top-tail-only samples. Forbes 400, top incomes, the top 135 metro areas, bestsellers and xmin-selected tails all are.
3. xmin and estimator dependence. The same data give 0.3-1.2 for fires, 0.95-1.20 for words, 1.09-1.49 for wealth, 0.5-1.0 for superflares and 0.81-1.40 for web hits. OLS rank regressions are biased.
4. Freedom to choose the variable. If q is proportional to k^d, then zeta_q = zeta_k/d, so any power-law variable can be rescaled to zeta = 1 by choosing its power after the fact. Earthquake rupture area (zeta about 1) versus moment or energy (zeta about 0.63) is the clearest case, including the repo's own re-expression. Only additive, conserved resources declared in advance should count.
5. Non-independence. Several city rows, and Clauset and Newman, reuse the same datasets.
6. Representative values. The summary uses one value per row, chosen by me, and the summary statistics are sensitive to those choices.
7. Non-resource variables. Degrees, counts of distinct things (species per genus, papers authored, active electrodes) and nested quantities (river drainage area) appear in the standard compilations and are flagged.

Omitted, and why:
- Bank sizes: no exponent retrieved. Janicki & Prescott find neither lognormal nor Pareto fits the whole range; Goddard et al. reject Zipf for the upper tail.
- Moon craters: diameter is not a held resource.
- Species-abundance distributions: lognormal or log-series, with no power-law exponent to report.
- Snow avalanches, stock-market capitalization, Kuiper-belt objects: not checked.
- Gabaix's formula zeta = 1/(1 - s_min/mean): derived, not found in the retrieved text of Gabaix (1999). The Blank & Solomon critique was verified through Malevergne et al. (2013).

## Pattern

Does zeta cluster at 1? Only partly, and much less than a law would require.

Distribution:
- Across 47 rows, using one representative value per row: median about 1.0, interquartile range about 0.65-1.36, range 0.1 to 3.3. Only about 30% of rows lie within +/-0.1 of 1 and about 40% within +/-0.25.
- Restricted to the 39 additive resource variables (stocks, fluxes and events): median 1.00, interquartile range about 0.65-1.30, 36% within +/-0.1.
- The least cherry-picked set, Clauset 2009's 24 datasets, does not center on 1. Converted values have a median of about 1.3, an interquartile range of about 1.0-1.7, and only 2-3 of 24 within +/-0.1. Newman 2005's resource-like rows have a median of about 1.2.

By class:
- Stocks (20 rows): cluster nearest 1. Median about 1.01, interquartile range about 0.90-1.13, about 45-50% within +/-0.1.
  - Stocks built by proportional growth with entry and exit come closest: US firms 1.06, European firms 0.89-1.00, US metro areas 1.005, natural cities about 1, word tokens 0.95-1.2, surnames 0.94. So do the ocean biomass spectrum (1.04) and aquatic size spectra (1.015).
  - But the same class also gives clear exceptions: administrative-city top tails 1.3-1.4 (zeta = 1 rejected), Forbes wealth 1.09-1.49, income 1.5-2.5 (a flux), gene families 1-3, and mutual funds with a lognormal tail and no power law at all.
  - Stocks from other mechanisms are often far from 1: trees 0.375, galaxy stellar mass 0.47, islands about 0.65, the stellar IMF 1.35 above the turnover and 0.3 below it, lakes 1.14 (percolation theory predicts 1.055), and simulated dark-matter subhalos 0.9.
- Events and cascades (16 rows): median about 0.8, and most lie below 1 (top-heavy), commonly 0.1-0.7.
  - Below 1: tropical-cyclone dissipation about 0-0.25, rain 0.17-0.4, wildfires 0.3-0.75, war deaths 0.53, hard X-ray flare energy about 0.5, seismic moment or energy 0.60-0.68, rock fragments 0.65-0.81, bolides 0.90.
  - Near 1: GOES soft X-ray fluence 1.03, Kepler superflares about 1 (or 0.5 at 1-min cadence), and earthquake rupture area 1.0. Rupture area is a re-expression of b about 1 and is not independent of the moment row.
  - Above 1: landslides 1.4, terrorism 1.4, blackouts 1.3.
  - Several event classes have theories that predict zeta not equal to 1: mean-field branching 1/2, fractal-diffusive SOC energy 2/3, Dohnanyi cascade 5/6, Kagan's argued beta = 1/2.
- Degree and count variables (8 rows) do not cluster at 1 (median about 1.4, range 0.43-3.3) and should be excluded from any test.

So yes, stocks with proportional growth cluster nearer 1 than events and cascades, but with large exceptions inside the favored class.

Theory checks (verified):
- Malevergne, Saichev & Sornette (2013) show that Gibrat growth gives zeta = 1 only under a balance condition: incumbent net growth, including exits, must equal growth of investment in entrants. Otherwise zeta differs from 1. They interpret zeta = 1 as the signature of maximum sustainable growth under a resource limit. That makes zeta = 1 a boundary case, not a generic attractor.
- Gabaix's limit (s_min/mean -> 0) was criticized by Blank & Solomon (2000) as incompatible with a finite number of units.
- Schwarzkopf & Farmer show relaxation to the Zipf steady state can be too slow to matter.

My synthesis (not a verified claim):
- The defensible mathematical statement is that zeta = 1 is the unique scale-free allocation in which resource per log-size decade is constant, so neither the lower nor the upper cutoff dominates the total.
- Every observed zeta < 1 system has an explicit upper cutoff: tropical-cyclone basin size, galaxy M*, the corner moment, finite world population for wars. Every zeta > 1 system has a lower cutoff.
- On this evidence, orthopolity in its log form works as a null or reference allocation: approximately realized by growth-driven stocks and by a few event classes, and violated by most cascades.
- 'Deviations are imposed by constraints' is testable only where the constraint predicts the deviation in advance, as in Dohnanyi's 5/6, percolation's 96/91, the trophic relation zeta = 1 - log TE/log PPMR, and Malevergne et al.'s balance condition. In those cases the predicted values are generally not 1.
- The compiled evidence does not support calling zeta = 1 a general natural law.
