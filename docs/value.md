# Why a limited scientific investigation is worthwhile

Read alongside the [critical assessment](criticism.md), [evidence ledger](evidence.md),
and [manuscript](paper.md).

## What is useful

The distinction between counting objects and counting their resources is a clear way to
interpret size spectra. It forces assumptions that are easy to omit: the additive resource,
the object being sampled, the coordinate, the reference measure, and the observation domain.
It explains why equal allocation and unequal counts can coexist without implying that the
allocation actually occurs.

The inverse-cost identity is established mathematics and has substantial ecological precedent.
Its value here is exposition and disciplined testing. It is not a newly discovered law,
a generic measure of maximal inequality, or a replacement for ecological mechanisms.

## The contribution of this repository

The revised manuscript provides:

- A formulation that covers discrete, linear, and logarithmic allocation without treating
  them as equivalent hypotheses.
- Explicit conditions for obtaining a power law, and counterexamples showing why conservation
  or independent inputs alone are insufficient.
- An ensemble counterexample: zero average slope does not imply a flat average resource profile.
- Reproducible exploratory analyses, including negative and unresolved results, with corrections
  to computational errors and metadata-dependent inference.
- A direct audit of the original manuscript rather than a verdict based only on later paraphrases.

This is enough for a useful research note or synthesis. Publication novelty remains a question
for a targeted literature comparison and peer review; it cannot be established by a new name.

## A worthwhile next empirical question

For a specified population and independently justified resource, does the complete resource
profile lie within a scientifically meaningful band on a declared size range? If a mechanism
predicts systematic departures, do those predictions transfer to a new sample?

A useful study would need raw measurements or trustworthy bin totals, sampling/exposure
information, validated size bounds and estimands, and an uncertainty model reflecting its
design. It would compare predictions against established ecological models rather than merely
against a flat line.

## Decision

Keep the mathematical and methodological investigation. Treat a new predictive experiment as
additional work requiring a justified hypothesis and suitable data. Do not infer that failure
of a universal version makes every conditional application uninteresting; equally, do not
treat a familiar central slope as confirmation of the universal idea.
