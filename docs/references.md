# References

Works the [concept](concept.md), [value](value.md), and [criticism](criticism.md) documents
depend on. Grouped by the role they play in the argument rather than alphabetically.

> Bibliographic details are given to the best available accuracy but **should be verified
> against the originals before any of this is submitted anywhere.**

## The closest prior art — equipartition of a measured resource

The strongest precedent for orthopolity, and the positive control for the programme.

- **Damuth, J. (1981).** Population density and body size in mammals. *Nature* 290, 699–700.
  — The original size–density power law, $N \propto M^{-3/4}$.
- **Nee, S., Read, A. F., Greenwood, J. J. D., & Harvey, P. H. (1991).** The relationship between
  abundance and body size in British birds. *Nature* 351, 312–313. — Coins "energetic equivalence
  rule": total energy use per species is independent of body size. This is orthopolity.
- **Isaac, N. J. B., Storch, D., & Carbone, C. (2013).** The paradox of energy equivalence.
  *Global Ecology and Biogeography* 22, 1–5. — Where and why it fails; observed exponents cluster
  nearer −0.5 to −0.6 than −0.75. Essential reading before claiming generality.
- **West, G. B., Brown, J. H., & Enquist, B. J. (1997).** A general model for the origin of
  allometric scaling laws in biology. *Science* 276, 122–126. — Derives the 3/4 exponent from
  network geometry: an $\alpha$ predicted rather than fitted, which is the standard to match.

## Scale invariance, dimension, and the geometric argument

- **Mandelbrot, B. B. (1982).** *The Fractal Geometry of Nature.* W. H. Freeman. — Box-counting
  dimension; $N(l) \propto l^{-D}$ is the essay's boxes argument.
- **Mandelbrot, B. (1953).** An informational theory of the statistical structure of language.
  In *Communication Theory*. — Information-theoretic derivation of Zipf's law; origin of the
  Zipf–Mandelbrot form $f \propto (\rho + b)^{-\alpha}$ referenced in the essay.

## Generating mechanisms — the alternatives orthopolity would displace

- **Newman, M. E. J. (2005).** Power laws, Pareto distributions and Zipf's law.
  *Contemporary Physics* 46(5), 323–351. — The standard survey; catalogues the competing
  mechanisms. The single most useful orientation document.
- **Mitzenmacher, M. (2004).** A brief history of generative models for power law and lognormal
  distributions. *Internet Mathematics* 1(2), 226–251. — On how easily power law and lognormal
  are confused.
- **Simon, H. A. (1955).** On a class of skew distribution functions. *Biometrika* 42, 425–440.
- **Yule, G. U. (1925).** A mathematical theory of evolution. *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 213,
  21–87.
- **Barabási, A.-L., & Albert, R. (1999).** Emergence of scaling in random networks.
  *Science* 286, 509–512. — Preferential attachment; the model the essay proposes to reinterpret.
- **Zipf, G. K. (1949).** *Human Behavior and the Principle of Least Effort.* Addison-Wesley.

## Constraint-based and maximum-entropy accounts — the nearest existing programme

Orthopolity is a constraint-first rather than mechanism-first account. This literature already
does that, and is where the framework should be positioned.

- **Jaynes, E. T. (1957).** Information theory and statistical mechanics.
  *Physical Review* 106, 620–630.
- **Frank, S. A. (2009).** The common patterns of nature. *Journal of Evolutionary Biology*
  22, 1563–1585. — Common distributions as consequences of constraints and invariances rather
  than mechanisms. The closest methodological sibling.
- **Visser, M. (2013).** Zipf's law, power laws and maximum entropy. *New Journal of Physics*
  15, 043021. — Power laws from a constraint on $\langle \ln x \rangle$.

## Statistical methodology — non-negotiable for any empirical claim

- **Clauset, A., Shalizi, C. R., & Newman, M. E. J. (2009).** Power-law distributions in
  empirical data. *SIAM Review* 51(4), 661–703. [arXiv:0706.1062](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1062)
  — MLE, KS goodness-of-fit, bootstrap $p$-values, likelihood-ratio tests. Log-log least squares
  is not acceptable evidence.
- **Alstott, J., Bullmore, E., & Plenz, D. (2014).** powerlaw: a Python package for analysis of
  heavy-tailed distributions. *PLoS ONE* 9(1), e85777. — Reference implementation of the above.
- **Stumpf, M. P. H., & Porter, M. A. (2012).** Critical truths about power laws.
  *Science* 335, 665–666. — A power law needs mechanism *and* statistical validation.
- **Broido, A. D., & Clauset, A. (2019).** Scale-free networks are rare.
  *Nature Communications* 10, 1017. — The explanandum may be less universal than assumed.

## Cosmology — why the "third cosmological principle" framing fails

- **Sylos Labini, F., Montuori, M., & Pietronero, L. (1998).** Scale-invariance of galaxy
  clustering. *Physics Reports* 293, 61–226. — The fractal-universe position.
- **Hogg, D. W., et al. (2005).** Cosmic homogeneity demonstrated with luminous red galaxies.
  *Astrophysical Journal* 624, 54–58.
- **Scrimgeour, M. I., et al. (2012).** The WiggleZ Dark Energy Survey: the transition to
  large-scale cosmic homogeneity. *MNRAS* 425, 116–134. — Homogeneity above ~70–80 Mpc/h;
  scale invariance does not survive to cosmological scales.
